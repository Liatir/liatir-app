PREDICT AND SIMULATE PROTEIN STRUCTURES

Two well-studied human proteins whose real 3D shape is known from
experiments, so you can compare what the AI predicts with reality.

- Ubiquitin: a small protein (76 amino acids) that cells use to tag other
  proteins for recycling.
- Carbonic anhydrase 2: an enzyme (260 amino acids) blocked by
  acetazolamide, a drug used for glaucoma. The drug binds it very tightly
  (about 12 nanomolar).


THE FILES

ubiquitin_sequence.fasta
carbonic_anhydrase_2_sequence.fasta
  The amino-acid sequence of each protein, on one line ready to copy: the
  only input the structure AI needs.

acetazolamide_drug.smi
  The drug written as SMILES, a one-line text description of its atoms and
  bonds: CC(=O)Nc1nnc(S(N)(=O)=O)s1

ubiquitin_crystal_structure_1UBQ.pdb
carbonic_anhydrase_2_with_acetazolamide_3HS4.pdb
  The real structures measured by X-ray crystallography. The second one
  shows the drug sitting in the enzyme's pocket.


WHAT TO TRY

1. 3D Structure Viewer - open either .pdb file to explore a real structure.

2. Structure Prediction (Boltz-2 or Protenix) - add a protein and paste the
   sequence from ubiquitin_sequence.fasta (the second line). When asked
   about the alignment, accept prediction from the sequence alone, so the
   run stays on your computer. Then compare the predicted structure with
   ubiquitin_crystal_structure_1UBQ.pdb in the 3D Structure Viewer.

3. Protein-Ligand Affinity (Boltz-2) - add a protein with the sequence from
   carbonic_anhydrase_2_sequence.fasta and a ligand with the SMILES from
   acetazolamide_drug.smi. A drug this strong should come out as a likely
   binder. Compare the predicted pose with the 3HS4 structure.

4. Molecular Relaxation, then Molecular Dynamics (OpenMM) - use
   ubiquitin_crystal_structure_1UBQ.pdb. Relaxation gently removes strain
   from the structure; dynamics simulates how it moves for a few
   picoseconds. Keep the default settings, or add water around it.

Boltz-2 and Protenix need a computer with an NVIDIA graphics card (Linux, or
Windows through WSL2). The AI Models page shows what your computer can run.


WHERE THIS DATA COMES FROM

Structures: Protein Data Bank entries 1UBQ and 3HS4 (RCSB PDB, CC0 public
domain), unchanged.
Sequences: the 1UBQ entry and UniProt P00918 (CC BY 4.0).
Drug: acetazolamide, as used in Liatir's own Boltz-2 validation.
