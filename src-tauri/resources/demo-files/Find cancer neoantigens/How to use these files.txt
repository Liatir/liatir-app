FIND CANCER NEOANTIGENS

A neoantigen is a small piece of a mutated protein that only tumor cells
carry. If a patient's immune system can "see" it, it can become the target of
a personalized cancer vaccine or cell therapy. Whether it is seen depends on
the patient's HLA types: the molecules that display protein pieces on the
cell surface, different from person to person.


THE FILES

tumor_mutations_HCC1395.vcf
  40 real mutations from HCC1395, a breast cancer cell line, already
  annotated with their effect on proteins (by VEP) and with RNA expression.
  The file holds both the tumor and the matching normal sample.

candidate_peptides.csv
  12 short protein pieces (peptides) to test directly: published
  neoantigens from the KRAS G12D, KRAS G12V and TP53 R175H cancer
  mutations, the matching normal pieces for comparison, and three virus
  pieces known to be displayed by HLA-A*02:01 (positive controls).

KRAS_normal_and_G12D_proteins.fasta
  The whole KRAS protein, normal and with the G12D mutation, one of the most
  common mutations in pancreatic, colon and lung cancer.


WHAT TO TRY

1. Neoantigen Prioritization (pVACseq)
   - VCF: tumor_mutations_HCC1395.vcf
   - Tumor sample: HCC1395_TUMOR_DNA
   - Normal sample: HCC1395_NORMAL_DNA
   - HLA alleles (this patient's own): HLA-A*29:02,HLA-B*45:01,HLA-B*82:02
   Keep the other defaults. It takes about 5 to 10 minutes on a laptop and
   ranks one candidate for each of the 40 mutations; about 20 reach the
   best tier, "Pass".

2. MHC-I Epitope Prediction (MHCflurry), peptide table
   - File: candidate_peptides.csv, input type "Peptide table"
   - HLA alleles: HLA-A*02:01,HLA-A*11:01,HLA-C*08:02
   The virus controls come out on top, with the 10-letter KRAS peptides on
   HLA-A*11:01 - the normal one too, because being displayed alone does not
   make a peptide tumor-specific. Now look at GADGVGKSA (KRAS G12D) on
   HLA-C*08:02: strongly displayed, while the normal GAGGVGKSA is not. That
   difference is what makes a neoantigen.

3. MHC-I Epitope Prediction, protein FASTA
   - File: KRAS_normal_and_G12D_proteins.fasta, input type "Protein FASTA"
   - Same alleles as above.
   Most top peptides appear in both proteins, so they are not
   tumor-specific. Look for those that appear only in KRAS_G12D.

These are research predictions, not a validated vaccine design.


WHERE THIS DATA COMES FROM

tumor_mutations_HCC1395.vcf: 40 records chosen from the official pVACseq
example data shipped with pVACtools 7.1.2 (Griffith Lab, BSD 3-Clause Clear
License), across its range of candidate tiers.
candidate_peptides.csv: peptides published in Wang et al., Cancer Immunology
Research 2016 (KRAS on HLA-A*11:01), Tran et al., New England Journal of
Medicine 2016 (KRAS G12D on HLA-C*08:02) and Hsiue et al., Science 2021
(TP53 R175H), plus classic influenza, CMV and EBV epitopes.
KRAS sequence: UniProt P01116 (CC BY 4.0), with glycine 12 changed to
aspartate for the mutant.
